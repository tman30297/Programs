# Green Computing & Data Center Energy Efficiency
## Research Report | March 2026

## Executive Summary

Green computing (also called green IT or ICT Sustainability) focuses on environmentally sustainable computing practices. With data centers consuming ~1.5% of global electricity (~415 TWh in 2024) and projected to double by 2030, energy efficiency has become a critical priority.

## Current State of Data Center Energy Consumption

### Global Numbers (2024-2026)
- **Global data center electricity consumption**: ~415 TWh in 2024 (1.5% of global electricity)
- **IEA Projection**: Could reach ~945 TWh by 2030 (under central scenario)
- **AI workload impact**: Major driver of growth; AI data centers can consume as much electricity as 100,000 households
- **US consumption**: 4.6-9.1% of US generation projected by 2030 (EPRI estimate)
- **US demand**: Expected to double to 35 GW by 2030 (from 17 GW in 2022)

## Key Green Computing Strategies

### 1. Hardware Lifecycle Management
- **Manufacturing impact**: PC manufacturing accounts for 70% of natural resources used in a PC's lifecycle
- **延长使用寿命**: Prolonging equipment lifetime is the biggest contribution to green computing
- **Modularity**: Upgradable, modular designs reduce need for complete replacements

### 2. Data Center Design (DOE Best Practices)
1. **IT systems optimization**
2. **Environmental conditions management**
3. **Air management**
4. **Cooling systems** (can account for 30%+ at enterprise sites vs ~7% at hyperscale)
5. **Electrical systems efficiency**

### 3. Software & Algorithmic Optimization
- **Algorithm efficiency**: Switching from linear to hashed search can reduce resource usage dramatically
- **Geographic routing**: Route traffic to data centers with cheaper/cleaner electricity (MIT research showed potential 40% savings)
- **Weather-based load shifting**: Route traffic away from warm-climate data centers to reduce AC use

### 4. Virtualization
- Combine multiple physical systems into virtual machines on fewer powerful systems
- Allow servers to enter low-power sleep states when not busy

## Industry Initiatives & Regulations

### Major Programs
- **Energy Star** (EPA, 1992): Voluntary labeling for energy-efficient equipment
- **EPEAT**: Electronic Product Environmental Assessment Tool - 51 criteria for "greener" computing
- **The Green Grid**: Global consortium advancing data center energy efficiency (founded 2007)
- **Green500**: Supercomputer energy efficiency rankings (MFLOPS/watt)
- **iMasons Climate Accord** (2022): Industry cooperation on reducing carbon in digital infrastructure

### Policy Targets
- **EU**: Climate-neutral data centers by 2030
- **US Federal**: Executive Order requiring EPEAT for computer purchases

## Emerging Trends (2025-2026)

### AI-Driven Growth Challenge
- AI workloads are accelerating high-performance server deployment
- Higher power density = increased strain on grids
- Water consumption for cooling becoming a regulatory concern

### Green Digital Governance
- EU combining Green Deal + Digital Europe Programme
- Using ICT to enable sustainability across sectors (precision agriculture, transport, energy)
- E-government services reducing paper/c emissions

### Infrastructure Shifts
- Migration from enterprise on-premises to hyperscale/colocation (74% of US server energy by 2023)
- Edge computing growth tied to 5G and IoT expansion

## Key Metrics & Benchmarks

- **DCIE** (Data Center Infrastructure Efficiency): Industry standard metric
- **PUE** (Power Usage Effectiveness): Ratio of total facility energy to IT equipment energy
- **SPECpower**: First industry benchmark measuring power vs performance for servers
